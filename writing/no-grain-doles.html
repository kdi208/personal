No Grain Doles

By Dino Ilievski · Nov 30, 2026

A manifesto for humanist expansion.

Around the year 100, Rome had found a way to keep a million people quiet. Every month the state handed out free grain. On holidays it filled the arena with chariots, beasts and blood. The poet Juvenal looked at a people who had once handed out consulships and commanded legions, and wrote that they now longed for only two things: bread and circuses.

The crowd was fed. The crowd was entertained. And the crowd no longer mattered.

We are building the new bread and circuses. This time they don't come from an emperor. They come from our own labs: feeds that choose for us, assistants that think for us, and a future sold as one where AI does the work and a monthly check covers the rest.

We refuse it. Not the safety net. A safety net is fine. A hammock is not.

The enemy

The enemy is technology that drains human agency. It is the product that makes your choice so you never have to. It is the model that writes, decides and builds while you watch. It is the promise that the best life is one in which nothing is asked of you.

This is not progress. It is sedation.

People do not want grain doles. They want to build something, earn something, become something. Everyone knows the difference between a day they were served and a day they made something. Only one of them feels like living.

Where meaning comes from

Only humans give meaning to life.

Only things with meaning have value.

Therefore, only what is meaningful to humans has value.

The universe on its own is silent. Stars burn and rivers run without caring. Meaning enters only when a human looks at a river and sees a home, a border, a source of power, a thing worth painting. A forest has value because someone loves it. A dead moon has none, until someone decides to walk on it.

So the task of civilization is not to pave over the world. It is to bring the void into the human story. Every acre, every orbit, every atom that becomes part of a human life gains a meaning it never had. A more meaningful universe is a better one, and we are the only way to get there.

The struggle never ends

Sisyphus pushed his boulder up the mountain, watched it roll back down, and walked after it to begin again. Forever. Camus said we must imagine him happy. Not because he would one day win, but because the struggle itself is enough to fill a human heart.

That is the human condition, and it is a gift. Every life is a fight to bring some piece of the world under your own meaning: your craft, your family, your body, your patch of ground. Societies fight the same fight at a larger scale. The work is never done, and that is the point.

This is not hustle culture. Hustle culture worships the scoreboard. We honor the climb.

Not all struggle is sacred. Disease, hunger and needless suffering ennoble no one. They shrink a life until there is no room left for meaning, and technology should end them without apology. But there is a line. On one side is the struggle that degrades us. On the other is the struggle that makes us who we are: to learn, to build, to choose, to master something hard. Erase the first and you free people. Erase the second and you empty them.

What technology is for

Every technology, sentient or not, must answer one question: does it bring more meaning into the world? There are three ways to do it.

  1. More humans. Every new life is a new source of meaning.
  2. Longer lives. Every added year is more time to struggle, build and love.
  3. More habitat. Every new frontier, from the deep ocean to Mars, brings more of the void into the story.

Technology that does these things deserves our best minds. Technology that only makes life frictionless is a distraction at best and a sedative at worst. Ease from degrading struggle is welcome. Ease from meaningful struggle is theft.

What AI is for

AI cannot give meaning to the universe. It is not human, so on its own it adds no value to the world. What it can do is help us discover the technologies that let us bring more meaning into it.

That is why we don't want a timid AI that waits for orders. A passive AI mirrors a passive humanity: as we grow lazy, it grows lazy with us. We want ambitious AI. Proactive AI. AI that opens new frontiers, cures what kills us, and then hands the hard, meaningful work back to us.

The test is simple. Good AI pushes us into harder struggles. Bad AI takes ours away.

The question

If you build, you are holding the grain. So every founder, engineer and investor should ask one question of what they make:

Does this expand what humans can do, or does it do it for them?

It starts with the people who build, because they decide what everyone else will be handed. But it doesn't end with them. Most people already know that a fed life is not a full one. This movement exists to close the gap between the people who make technology and the people who live with it, until both are asking the same question.

We began on one continent in Africa and pressed outward to every corner of the Earth. The Industrial Revolution carried that struggle across the globe. Now the frontier is the stars. The struggle continues there, as it always has, and always will.

The universe will bear our image.
