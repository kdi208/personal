The Princeps Thesis

By Dino Ilievski · Oct 22, 2026

Princeps backs the founders who go first. This is what I look for in them, and why.

No one will be carried

Everyone is complaining about data centers. Everyone is afraid of becoming the permanent underclass. They are right. That is exactly where most of them are headed.

The road through the singularity to Omega demands a great divergence in outcomes. We do not all contribute equally, and we will not all end up equal. No one will be carried. There will be no universal basic income, and no attempt to drag the classes back together.

What is offered instead is larger than comfort: a part in the triumph of humanity over nature. That triumph will be led by a few founders. Princeps exists to find them, and to help them say out loud where they are going.

Those who go first

Today's tech leaders can see where the curve goes, so they reach for the mask: a UBI pilot here, "universal basic compute" there, a pledge to hand everyone a slice of the equity. Anything to keep the crowd calm. But the crowd is shrewd. People know a payoff meant to keep them quiet when they see one.

Augustus wore the mask first. After the civil wars he refused the titles of king and dictator and called himself princeps, the first citizen, while he held the legions, the treasury and the final word. Today's version is the same trick: hold the compute, the capital and the models, and call it sharing.

The Republic had an older meaning. The princeps senatus was the senator whose name stood first on the roll. He held no extra power by law. He spoke first, and his voice set the tone of the debate. His standing came from merit, not command.

That is the order I believe in. Society is run by those who go first, not by birth or by force. Who decides who is first? No one. The work does. Princeps keeps what outlived Rome, that someone goes first, and what Augustus added, that the first is in charge. The difference is that we say it out loud.

Rand called these people prime movers. Nietzsche called them the Übermensch. Same type: those who build, make their own values, and live for greatness instead of the herd's comfort. Rand's mistake was letting Galt go on strike. The princeps does not get to quit.

What I look for in a founder

  1. You build what did not exist. Not a feature, not a copy with better marketing. Something the world ends up depending on, whether it likes it or not.
  2. Your work points at Omega. You can say how what you are building moves humanity further past its limits: in biology, in machines, or in the fusion of the two.
  3. You can tell me the future without a deck. Ask you what the world looks like in twenty years because you existed, and you answer from memory, with conviction, in your own words.
  4. You use the machines to think harder, not to stop thinking. AI should make you sharper. A founder who outsources their judgment to a model has already handed over the company.
  5. You say the obvious out loud. No mask. You tell your team, your investors and the public what you are really building and what it will cost.
  6. You do not quit. No strike, no early exit to a quiet life. When it gets hard, you stay in charge.
  7. You want to be first, not merely funded. The round is fuel. It is not the product.

You are probably not the princeps

Now the part founders will not like. You read this and cast yourself as the princeps. Most of you are not.

A thin wrapper on someone else's model is not going first. A fund that piles into whatever rose last quarter is not going first. A company whose real product is the next round is fundraising dressed up as a company.

The singularity will sort this faster than any market. Most of you are the crowd. You just have better seats.

You will ask how anyone claims a place if the machines do the work. The question is a confession: you were waiting to be carried. The answer is the same as on every frontier. Do what the machines cannot do yet. Attach yourself to someone going first. Own a piece of what is being built. Or build. The frontier moves. It does not close.

And if what you want is a guarantee, the answer is no. A guaranteed place is a handout, and handouts do not build the future.

A vector, not a place

Omega is Teilhard de Chardin's name for the final point of evolution, minus his theology. In our century it has a shape: the fusion of biology and machine. Omega may never be reached. It does not need to be. A line needs an end to have a direction.

So Princeps does not promise anyone a place to arrive. It offers a vector, and a vector has two things: magnitude and direction. The magnitude is the size of your work. The direction is Omega.

Along that line I want three things to hold. The struggle continues after the singularity, because meaning is earned and never issued. Humans use the machines to raise themselves, never to stop thinking. And humanity builds an accurate simulation of itself, so that we finally see what we are and where we are going.

There is no participation prize. Small work pointed at Omega counts. Vast work pointed anywhere else is drift.

What Princeps does for those who go first

I do not found the companies. I find the founders who go first and help them tell the world where they are going.

  • Vision and story. We find the myth your company is built on and set it in words you will repeat for a decade.
  • One company per market. I never work with your competitor. If I am in your corner, I am only in yours.
  • In person, always. The real conversation does not happen on a video call.
  • The right people. Introductions to the hires, specialists and investors who should be part of what you are building.
  • In the open. Every company I work with is named on the Princeps site. The portfolio is the argument.

Build, or take your seat

The future is coming whether you are ready or not. A place in it will not be handed to anyone for merely existing. It must be claimed, and it must be struggled for.

No one owes you a place. Build, or take your seat. If you are building, find me.
