There Will Be No Grain Dole

By Dino Ilievski · Oct 21, 2026

You won't get a paycheck. You get a front-row seat to our apotheosis.

No grain dole

Everyone is complaining about data centers. Everyone is afraid of becoming the permanent underclass. They are right. That is exactly where they are headed.

We need a new class of leaders with the nerve to say the obvious.

We do not all contribute equally, and we will not all end up equal. The road through the singularity to Omega demands a great divergence in outcomes. No one will be carried.

There will be no universal basic income. There will be no attempt to drag the classes back together.

What is offered instead is larger than comfort: the chance to take part in the triumph of humanity over nature.

The mask

Today's tech leaders will not say this. They can see where the curve goes, so they reach for the mask: a UBI pilot here, "universal basic compute" there, a pledge to hand everyone a slice of the equity. Anything to keep the crowd calm.

But the crowd is shrewd. Rome fed its mob free grain to keep it quiet, and people still know a grain dole when they see one.

The mask is not new. Augustus wore it first. After the civil wars, he refused the titles of king and dictator and called himself princeps, the first citizen. In truth, he held the legions, the treasury and the final word. The Republic kept its forms, and he became its monarch.

Today's version is the same trick. Hold the compute, the capital and the models, and call it sharing.

So drop the mask. Tell them the truth: there is a struggle ahead for everyone, a necessary strife before the promised land of the singularity, and beyond it, Omega.

Princeps

Every society is led by a few. The Romans had a word for them: princeps, the first.

Under the Republic, the princeps senatus was the senator whose name stood first on the roll. He held no extra power by law. He spoke first, and his voice set the tone of the debate. His standing came from merit, not command.

I take princeps for a new kind of order. Call it princeps-ism: society is run by those who go first. They are not rulers by birth or by force. Who decides who is first? No one. The work does.

Princeps-ism keeps the core that outlived Rome: someone goes first. And it keeps what Augustus added: the first is in charge. The difference is that we say it out loud.

The lineage

Rand called them prime movers. Nietzsche called them the Übermensch. Same type: those who build, make their own values, and live for greatness instead of the herd's comfort. Rand's mistake was letting Galt go on strike. The princeps does not get to quit. The princeps is that type, awake to itself and in charge.

Where are they going? To Omega, Teilhard de Chardin's name for the final point of evolution, minus his theology. In our century it has a shape: the fusion of biology and machine. Omega may never be reached. It does not need to be. A line needs an end to have a direction.

You are probably not the princeps

Now the part founders will not like.

You read this and cast yourself as the princeps. Most of you are not. A thin wrapper on someone else's model is not going first. A fund that piles into whatever rose last quarter is not going first. A company whose real product is the next round is a grain dole with a cap table.

The princeps builds what did not exist and what everyone else ends up depending on. Count how many of those you have funded. Ask honestly whether you are one.

The singularity will sort this faster than any market. Most of you are the crowd. You just have better seats.

A place in the sun

First citizen implies there are others who are not first. Not everyone can be in charge of creating the future. The princeps is first because the work comes first.

The future is coming whether you are ready or not. A place in it will not be doled out like grain to anyone who happens to be a citizen. You will not be handed a place in the sun merely for existing as a human being.

You must claim it. You must struggle for it.

You will ask how, if the machines do the work. The question is a confession: you were waiting to be carried. The answer is the same as on every frontier. Do what the machines cannot do yet. Attach yourself to someone going first. Own a piece of what is being built. Or build. The frontier moves. It does not close.

And if what you want is a guarantee, the answer is no. A guaranteed place is a grain dole.

A vector, not a place

Princeps-ism does not give you a place to arrive. It gives you a vector, and a vector has two things: magnitude and direction. The magnitude is the size of your work. The direction is Omega.

There is no participation prize. Small work pointed at Omega counts. Vast work pointed anywhere else is drift.

No one owes you a place. Build, or take your seat.
